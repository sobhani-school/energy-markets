Quick start

1
Choose a network

Pick one from Start from, or start with a Blank canvas.

2
Run the market

Press 2 Run market. Prices and flows appear right away.

3
Change something

Click a bus, line or generator and edit it on the right. The market re-solves as you type.

4
Read the results

Use the tabs under the diagram for prices, dispatch, flows and payments.

What the tool solves. The cheapest dispatch that meets every load without breaking any line limit (a DC optimal power flow). Each bus's LMP is the dual variable of its power-balance constraint: how much total cost rises if that bus's load rises by 1 MW.

Choose a drawing tool above the diagram, or press its key.

Buses

  • Load in MW.
  • Optional demand bids: blocks of load (MW at a price), served only when the LMP is at or below the bid.
  • Bid prices can't rise from one block to the next.

Lines

  • Reactance x (per unit, 100 MVA base) decides how flow splits between paths.
  • Resistance r matters only with Losses on.
  • Limit in MW, enforced with Line limits on.

Generators

  • An offer curve of up to 5 blocks (MW at a price).
  • Block prices can't fall from one block to the next.
  • With several periods: an optional ramp limit in MW per period.

You can also edit everything in the Buses, Lines and Generators tables. Network checks on the right lists anything that stops the market from running; click a message to jump to the element.

Market settings Step 2

Line limits
Enforces each line's MW limit. A binding line creates congestion, and LMPs then differ by bus.
Losses
Adds I²R losses on each line. LMPs gain a loss component.
Reference bus
Its LMP is the energy component. Changing it shifts value between the energy, loss and congestion parts but never changes the LMPs.
VOLL
Value of lost load. Load that can't be served is priced at VOLL.
Periods
How many periods the day has, from 1 to 24.
Settlement
Single market, or Day-ahead + real-time.

Periods and ramp limits

  • Set Periods above 1 and the Buses tab shows a load profile: one multiplier per period, applied to every bus's base load.
  • Type into a period column to set one bus's load by hand. Reset all buses to base × profile undoes that.
  • Use the Period buttons above the diagram to step through the day.
All periods clear together in one optimization. A unit that can't ramp fast enough links neighbouring periods, so it can move prices both before and after the period where it binds.

Day-ahead and real-time

Set Settlement to Day-ahead + real-time. The market then clears twice. Switch the diagram between the two with the Market buttons above it.

Day-ahead

Clears on the network exactly as you built it: the forecast.

Real-time

Clears again after the Real-time events in the panel on the right.

Adding an event: choose a type, fill in its options, choose a period (or all periods), then press Add event.

Load change, all buses (%)Load change at a bus (MW)Random forecast error (± %)Unit outage or derateLine outageLine derate
Two-settlement rule. Day-ahead MWh are paid at day-ahead LMPs. In real time, only the deviation from the day-ahead schedule settles, at the real-time LMP. A unit that trips after clearing day-ahead buys back its schedule at the real-time price.

ComparePin a result as a baseline, change anything, and see what moved.
Why two surplus numbers? Dispatch & settlement shows the merchandising surplus for one period of one market. The Settlement statement covers the whole day: the sum over periods, plus the real-time balancing surplus in two-settlement mode. With one period and a single market they are equal. The statement's "Where the ISO surplus comes from" table shows each piece.

Saving and sharing

  • Your work is kept in this browser between visits.
  • Save / load gives a code with the whole network, its settings and real-time events. Share it with students, or paste one in to open it.
  • On the course website you can also download or open a .json file.

Model assumptions

  • DC power flow: voltages fixed at 1 pu, no reactive power.
  • One-hour periods, so MW and MWh are equal in settlements.
  • Same offers day-ahead and in real time. No unit commitment: no start-up costs or minimum outputs.
  • Ramp limits are lifted between two periods when a unit's available capacity changes, such as during an outage.
  • Breakpoints: if an LMP sits exactly at one, more than one price is valid. The +1 MW test flags these.
The code holds the whole network and its settings. Share it with students, or keep it to reopen later. Downloading a file works when the page is opened from your own website (for example GitHub Pages).
